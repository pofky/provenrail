I had no idea what my coding agents were actually doing, so I read a month of my own transcripts.

2,154 files. 3.78 GB. 124 seconds to parse.

942 sessions. 79,782 tool calls. 51,443 shell commands. 7,009 files written or edited.

$7,856.31.

Every Claude Code session writes a transcript to ~/.claude/projects and most people never open one. The whole picture of what your agents did, and what they cost, is already on your disk.

The part that got my attention was not the money. It replays your own commands back through a policy engine and tells you which ones would have been stopped. Mine: 29 refused, 483 that should have come to me first. Mostly deploy commands, deletes pointed outside the project, and 41 reads of .env.

On the cost being honest: it is an estimate at list price and it is a floor. Across the 49 sessions where Claude Code kept its own total, mine came in 12 per cent under. Fast mode and web search are billed in ways a transcript does not show. So it prints both numbers next to each other instead of inventing a multiplier.

Offline, no account, nothing leaves the machine. There is a --share mode that strips paths and hashes project names, and every command is reduced to verb and flags with the operands dropped, so you can post yours without leaking anything.
